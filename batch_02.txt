113	PART 1: CORE CONCEPTS & GAME OVERVIEW GAME OVERVIEW PART 1: CORE CONCEPTS GAME OVERVIEW Five thousand miles under the failing disc of Luada.
114	Dozens of kingdoms, tribes, and races, all knowing the world will end, and all too conflicted to act.
115	Cities sprawling around the rays of light that bring doom, but also power.
116	Mysteries of the past, waiting to be uncovered.
117	Whoever you are and whatever your story is, one thing is certain.
118	In Evershade, adventure will find you.
119	WHAT IS LANDS OF EVERSHADE?
120	Lands of Evershade is a campaign, cooperative, narrative board game for 1-5 players, inspired by classic tabletop role playing games.
121	To begin their journey, each player uses the Player’s Handbook to create a unique character, selecting from a wide range of races, professions, origins, paths, and other characteristics.
122	Then, a Party of these Characters sets upon one of several available Adventures that allow the Characters to make their way through gripping stories, fight Enemies on tactical maps, and explore various areas of Evershade.
123	In the process, the Characters advance and gather better equipment to handle even bigger challenges.
124	Each Adventure takes from 12-18 hours, and is most often divided into 3-6 hour Chapters, each of which with two 1-3 hour Acts.
125	Once a Character completes their final Adventure (Expert level Adventure), they can either embark on one of the deadly, dangerous Epic scenarios or retire, making way for a new generation of adventurers.
126	GOLDEN RULES Rounding values.
127	Each time you have to count fractional values, round them up to whole numbers.
128	Component limits.
129	Most components in the game have a limited supply.
130	If you are to draw, use, or place a component and all copies of it are already in play, ignore the effect.
131	Universal markers, HP dials, and dice are the only exception.
132	In rare cases, when you have run out of these components, find and use a substitute.
133	Order of effect resolution.
134	In the rare case where you must resolve multiple game effects at the same time, players may choose the order of resolution.
135	Detailed effects overwrite basic rules.
136	If you find effects on any component that in some way contradict the rules from this rulebook, the detailed effect takes precedence.
137	CORE CONCEPTS CHARACTERS Each player controls one Character during the game.
138	A Character is described with their Attributes, race, origin, profession and keywords.
139	They determine if your Character is a strong but asocial alf with a troubled past, a dextrous and dashing blank from a port town, a weak but magic-proficient shrewick, and many more.
140	Each Character may also be equipped with a multitude of different Items to aid them in their endeavours, have Action cards representing their Combat maneuvers and spells, and keep some brill on their person – the currency used in Evershade.
141	All of those components are stored on the Character board and Hotbar.
142	The excess that cannot fit into the slots is placed in the Pouch – the personal storage of a Character.
143	PARTY When adventurers join forces, they form a Party.
144	A Party consists of a min- imum of two Characters and potentially some Companions.
145	If playing solo, the Party will consist of a player Character traveling with a Partner – a stronger Companion to help them.
146	The Party fights and explores together, and makes their decisions about the story as a team.
147	This does not exclude the possibility of heated debates whether to help a wounded merchant on the road or rob them and leave them to die.
148	ADVENTURES In Lands of Evershade the Party may embark on three types of Adventures – Starting level for beginners, Advanced level for experienced Characters, and Expert level serving as a conclusion of the journey for veteran Characters.
149	During Adventures the Party will live through the story, traverse the land, fight their way through encounters, explore their backstories, Camp, gather Experience, and Advance their skills.
150	After an Expert level Adventure the Character may embark on the last, deadly Epic scenario or retire.
151	While playing an Adventure, keep in mind that you should use the com- ponents from the base game, as well as the components from the current Adventure – this includes the Storybook, the Secret Sheets and other com- ponents marked with an appropriate code (e.g.
152	“ItW” for the “Into the Whitewell”
153	Adventure).
154	CHAPTERS AND ACTS Each Adventure is divided into three smaller parts called Chapters.
155	Chapters mark the important story parts of an Adventure.
156	Chapters are further divided into Acts.
157	Each Act is a perfect fragment of the Adventure to play during a single game session and may take from 1 to 3 hours.
158	This depends on how thoroughly the players decide to explore, whether they have Companions in their Camp to interact with, and what additional interactions they play through as a part of their Personal Secrets.
159	After each Act, you will have an opportunity to save the game.
160	PART 1: CORE CONCEPTS & GAME OVERVIEW CORE CONCEPTS
161	In this section, you will find an overview on how to play the game.
162	This will come in the form of a brief description of all main systems and parts of the Adventure you may encounter.
163	The introduction here should be sufficient to begin playing, but if you need more detailed descriptions with examples, they might be found in “Part 3: Characters” and “Part 4: Detailed rules”.
164	The game is divided into two parts.
165	The first – Exploration – is facilitated with the Storybook and Secret Sheets.
166	This part includes traversing wilderness, exploring cities, tackling dungeons, solving puzzles, and engaging in social interactions.
167	During Exploration, the game always instructs you what to do next.
168	It might be reading an entry in the Storybook or taking an Action from a Secret Sheet.
169	The second part is Combat.
170	It is played on Secret Sheets with grid-based tactical maps as turn-based encounters.
171	In Combat, Characters and Enemies perform Activations in a specified order.
172	PART 1: CORE CONCEPTS & GAME OVERVIEW PLAYING THE GAME STORYBOOK The story in an Adventure is presented in a Storybook.
173	It contains num- bered entries with story descriptions and choices.
174	When navigating the Storybook, the entries will redirect you to other entries (e.g.
175	“Go to 100.”), instruct you to mark your decisions in the Story Tracker, progress Time and much more.
176	When making a decision, the Party should agree on one option.
177	If they cannot come to a conclusion, the Character with the Decision token makes the final call.
178	Some redirections or choices may require you to have proper keywords.
179	Entries may also contain Rolls.
180	For details on Storybook navigation go to page 15.
181	ROLLS Many Actions in this game are resolved with Rolls – you will roll twelve-sided dice (D12) to determine if your Action is successful or not.
182	Each time you are instructed to perform a Roll, choose one Character to do that.
183	Take a number of D12s according to the Attribute (ATT) of the Roll (STR, AGI, INT or CHA), and include modifiers – most often additional dice for having proper keywords.
184	Other Characters may Support the Roll by rolling their own dice, but only if they meet the requirements of the modifiers.
185	Each Roll has a set Difficulty shown by the icon.
186	Each die with a value equal to, or higher than the Difficulty counts as 1 Success.
187	A die with a value of 12 counts as 2 Successes.
188	You need to achieve as many Successes as described in the outcome of the Roll for it to be successful.
189	During Rolls, you may spend Fate to reroll dice.
190	If you fail a Roll or roll a 1 on a die, you will gain Fate .
191	For details on performing Rolls and Supporting them, go to page 16.
192	PLAYING THE GAME SECRET SHEETS Secret Sheets are numbered boards used to represent the environment and various scenes you will encounter during an Adventure.
193	The Storybook instructs you when to place a new Sheet or discard the one you no longer need.
194	The Story Tracker has room for two Secret Sheets – one on the left and the second on the right side.
195	All the unused Secret Sheets should be placed under the Cover Sheet to prevent accidental spoilers.
196	Each Secret Sheet has its special rules and Actions applying to this part of Exploration or Combat.
197	For details on Secret Sheets, go to page 18.
198	COMBAT Combats are played as turn-based encounters on a tactical grid.
199	During Combat each participant – Party Members or Enemies – will resolve their Activation.
